# Critique: OpenAI

## What it does well
OpenAI's site uses a lot of negative space and soft, almost editorial-magazine typography choices for a company whose product is fundamentally deeply technical — a deliberate choice to make AI feel approachable/human rather than intimidating. Strong use of large-format photography and soft color fields.

## What to borrow
- **Confidence in negative space** even when the underlying subject matter is complex — reinforces `design/LayoutSystem.md`'s whitespace/rhythm principles regardless of visual system chosen.
- **Editorial pacing** — content is paced like a magazine feature, not a spec sheet, even when explaining technical concepts. Useful reference for `branding/Storytelling.md`'s Problem → Approach → Impact case-study pacing — give each beat room to breathe rather than compressing everything into dense paragraphs.

## Where to diverge
OpenAI's softness (rounded forms, warm neutral tones, photography-driven) is close to the opposite of Mecha Typography's hard edges and schematic linework — this is a case of "borrow the pacing/spacing principle, not the visual language." Also worth noting: OpenAI's site is optimized for a mass, non-technical audience discovering AI for the first time — ManhnhaPortfolio's primary audience (`profiles/Recruiter.md`, `profiles/HiringManager.md`, `profiles/TechLead.md`) is smaller and more technically literate, so more density and specificity (real metrics, real architecture detail) is appropriate here in a way it wouldn't be on a mass-market AI product page.
