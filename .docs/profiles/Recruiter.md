# Persona: Recruiter

## Who they are
Non-technical or semi-technical talent-sourcing professional, screening many candidates. Not evaluating deep technical correctness — evaluating **fit, clarity, and signal strength**.

## Time on site
~10–20 seconds on first pass. If nothing legible registers in that window, they move on. A second, longer look (~1–2 min) only happens if the first pass earns it.

## What they need to see immediately (first viewport)
- A clear role/identity statement — "Data Science Engineer" (or equivalent), not a vague "I build cool things with AI."
- Enough specificity to mentally slot the candidate into a req: DS / ML / Data Viz / MLOps are the four areas ManhNha is confident in (per `foundation/TechnicalIdentity.md`) — these should be the first skills a recruiter's eye lands on, not buried in a long flat list.
- Signals of legitimacy: real project(s), real links (GitHub), not just claims.

## What convinces them to keep reading / reach out
- A skimmable Skills section with clear grouping (not a wall of tags) — see `docs/playbooks/SkillsSection.md`.
- At least one project they can understand the *headline* of without reading deeply — e.g. "Built an AI system that generates image captions" reads clearly even to a non-technical recruiter, before they see BLEU scores or architecture diagrams.
- A working, low-friction Contact path.

## What loses them
- Jargon-first framing with no plain-language translation.
- No clear "what role is this person looking for" signal — since target company/role type isn't locked yet (see `foundation/ProductRequirements.md`), the site should at minimum make the *domain* (Data Science / ML / AI engineering) unambiguous even if the exact job title target stays flexible.

## Assumption flagged
No specific target company/industry was set yet — this profile assumes a generalist tech-recruiter reading the site cold, without company-specific context. Revisit if a target company type is chosen later.
